# Pause Light: research

**Process behind the look:** Two lineages. (1) Annunciator panels: back-lit windows, each engraved with one alarm name, wired to a central board. Lamps are binary (lit or not), the legend is fixed text, and behaviour follows a standard sequence (ISA 18.1): flash plus horn on alarm, operator acknowledges, then steady lit until the condition clears. A lamp-test button lights everything. (2) DIN 1451 Engschrift: Prussian railway lettering (1905), standardised 1931, drawn on a coarse grid for compass and ruler, and cut as stencils and cast metal, never as printing type. Quirks: plain geometry, near-constant stroke, tight narrow forms, stencil-friendly construction.

**Colour, texture, composition:** Annunciators used few hues, each with a fixed meaning (aviation: red critical, amber caution, green advisory, white/blue in use), on dark panels so lit windows read as light, not paint. ISO 3864 safety colours are signal red, yellow, blue and green, with white and black as contrast; yellow/black 45-degree stripes mark hazards. Stencil-cut lettering means uniform strokes and flat fills, with no texture. Composition is a grid of equal windows, one message per window.

**Rules (5 to 10):**
1. Red appears only as the PAUSE lamp and gate markers. Never decoration, never a link.
2. Maximum three signal hues (red, yellow, blue) plus bone on black. No green.
3. A lamp has two states, off or lit. No gradients, glow limited to one soft halo.
4. One lamp event sequence: flash on arrival at a gate, steady while the human has not decided. Never loop.
5. Every status chip carries a text legend. Colour never speaks alone.
6. One message per row, in one condensed face, uppercase for statements only.
7. Strokes are uniform and corners square; no radius, no shadow, no italics.
8. Readouts use tabular figures so counters do not jitter.
9. Hazard stripes (yellow/black, 45 degrees) appear at most once, at the always-pause list.
10. Include a lamp-test moment on entry (all lamps light once), then rest.

**Named patterns:**
- Annunciator window / tile: one lit legend box per alarm.
- Master warning / master caution: the single summary lamp (the PAUSE lamp).
- Acknowledge: the act that stops flashing and holds steady.
- Lamp test: all lamps lit briefly to prove they work.
- Hazard stripe: alternating 45-degree yellow/black band.
- Engschrift: DIN 1451 condensed cut.
- Signal word: DANGER / WARNING / CAUTION panel (ISO 3864-2).

**Where this Direction risks a cliché:** "Dark dashboard with neon status colours" and giant uppercase condensed headlines are both common AI-landing tropes; avoid by keeping red scarce and using real legend text. Also, ISO blue means mandatory action, not "second model family"; a reader versed in standards may misread it, so label it.
