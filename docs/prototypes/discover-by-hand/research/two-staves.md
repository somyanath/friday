# Two Staves: research

**Process behind the look:** A Risograph is a stencil duplicator: a master is burned, wrapped round an ink drum, and ink is pushed through onto paper. It prints one colour per pass, so every extra colour sends the sheet back through. Ink is rice-bran oil, never truly dries, and soaks in over days. Engraved scores are the opposite discipline: fixed staff geometry, thin/thick line pairs, and a conductor's soft pencil layered on top by hand.

**Colour, texture, composition:** Riso gives few, translucent, saturated spot inks; overlap is chosen as overprint (inks mix) or knockout (cut out). Registration is never perfect, because the paper is gripped slightly differently each pass. Tone is made of grain, a stochastic dither ("grain-touch"): dots get denser, not darker. Expect uneven coverage, ink ghosts, and smudge. Score pages are mostly empty paper with dense horizontal lines; the conductor's marks sit above and between systems, in a different medium.

**Rules (5 to 10):**
1. Two inks only. Their overlap is the one allowed third colour and only where families agree.
2. Each ink is its own layer; offset layers by 0 to 3px, never more, and never rotate one.
3. Overlap multiplies (darker); never alpha-blend to a flat grey or mix a new hue.
4. Tone comes from grain or halftone dots, never smooth gradients. Solid fills stay slightly uneven.
5. Staff lines are five thin, evenly spaced lines; bar lines are thin and cross all five.
6. Text never sits in a box; it sits on, above or below a stave, as tempo words do.
7. Pencil is a third medium: grey, thin, wobbly, never in either ink, and it overlaps the print without registering to it.
8. One pause mark (fermata) per gate, no other ornament.
9. Pause marks and double barlines appear only at section ends or the three gates.
10. Misregistration moves slowly or not at all; a jitter reads as a glitch, not a print.

**Named patterns:**
- Fermata: arc over a dot, a held note or rest.
- Double barline: two thin lines marking a section end; thin-thick is the final barline.
- Rehearsal mark: boxed or circled letter or number, a place to start from.
- Tempo/expression marking: italic words (Allegro, Con gusto) above the stave.
- System, clef, bar number: the stave group, the opening sign, the number at bar start.
- Cut bar / cut on cue: pencilled out bars; "Q" marks a cut on a cue.
- Breath mark, cue notes, eyeglasses: conductor's pencil warnings.
- Overprint vs knockout, misregistration, grain-touch, ghosting, trapping: riso terms.

**Where this Direction risks a cliché:** "Neon riso with a serif" is a very familiar poster style; the Didone-italic plus two-ink look can become decoration. A score metaphor can stay costume if the staves carry no real structure (bars, rests, counts). Sideways scroll is also hard to skim on a phone.
