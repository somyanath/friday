# Blueprint Turnstile
**Idea:** A horizontal cyanotype drawing sheet that the reader walks along, left to right, through a pipeline of Friday's stages. Scroll is physically locked at each of the three taste gates, a bar across the sheet, until the reader clicks APPROVE. The reader does the job Somya does in Friday.
**Override:** none
**Look:** Two colours only: Prussian blue (#0B2A4A) ground, white for everything else, with white at 15 to 40% opacity for fine blueprint grid and hairlines. Type is one huge Swiss grotesk (Neue Haas or Inter Tight style), set flush-left in tight 160 to 280px headlines, with small 12px mono-spaced-feeling captions in the same family and tabular figures. The sheet is a continuous drawing: a single white rail runs the full width with dimension ticks and station numbers (01 to 07). Gates are tall white-outlined turnstile arms rotated 90 degrees across the rail. No images, no gradients, no shadows; an occasional white-line diagram on the grid. Asymmetric, with large blue voids.
**Signature moment:** The reader scrolls sideways (wheel, swipe or arrow key) and the sheet stops dead at a gate, the arm lowered across the rail. A terminal line types out beneath it: `> direction gate: 14 ideas → pick 3`. The reader clicks APPROVE, the arm rotates up with a mechanical tick, a stamp reads "APPROVED, S.M.", and the scroll frees. At the Variant gate, two columns of typed output compare live variants side by side; at the Ship gate the final line types `merge held: awaiting you`.
**How it says the three messages:**
- Taste: the page is a restrained two-colour drawing with scale, rhythm and type doing all the work, and the reader's clicks are the three places taste is exercised.
- Builds own tools: a typed `/friday <task>` route line opens the sheet, and a station on "curated: 162 pieces, each ruled keep, cut, merge or replace" shows what was reused and what was built.
- Helps teams: after the final gate, the rail continues out of frame and a typed line reads "same line, your team's work", turning the gate metaphor on the reader's own pipeline.
**Ending:** The last station is a final gate that opens by itself. The typed prompt `> what slows your team's workflow?` waits with a blinking cursor, and the white email and LinkedIn links sit under it as large grotesk lines, the end of the rail. On phones, the same sheet scrolls horizontally with a thumb-sized APPROVE button, and reduced motion shows the text pre-typed.
