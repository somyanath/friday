# Contact Sheet
**Idea:** The page is a photographer's proof sheet: rows of near-square frames on warm paper, with a red grease pencil crossing out the rejected ones and circling the keeper. Choosing is the page's whole subject, and the page does it in front of the reader.

**From the string:**
- Opening "89" → paper and ink hue at about 89 degrees: a lemony, aged cream (oklch 0.94 0.04 89), and frames shaped 8:9.
- Four separate "2"s → four frames in every row, which matches the four Variants.
- Three capital "E"s and three "X"s → exactly three grease-pencil gates; the X becomes the rejection mark.
- Alternating case, with lone lowercase runs ("aa", "ao") → a high-contrast serif set against a monospace, used in sharp alternation with nothing in between.
- Ends "dXR" → the page closes on one deliberate red mark rather than a footer.

**Look:** Paper cream, aubergine-black ink (hue about 320, nearly neutral), and a single vermilion pencil red used only for marks. Headlines in a high-contrast display serif (Fraunces or Newsreader) at large, uneven sizes. Captions, frame numbers and the `/friday` line in a mono (Martian Mono), like edge-code on film. Layout is a left-weighted, deliberately off-grid sheet: frames of different heights, a narrow gutter of film-edge numerals, and big margins on the right where the copy sits. No centred hero. Imagery is the frames themselves, built as cropped abstract UI fragments in Friday's own colours, with slight grain. No icons, no logo strip.

**Signature moment:** On scroll, a hand-drawn SVG pencil strokes an X through three of the four frames, one per gate, each with a short stroke-dashoffset draw. It then loops a wobbly circle around the fourth. Under reduced motion the marks are simply there.

**How it says the three messages:**
- Taste: the sheet's restraint, type pairing and single red accent are the argument, and the page demonstrates choosing as it goes.
- Builds own tools: a short "kept / cut / merged / replaced" tally, written as pencil tick marks beside the three source collections.
- Helps teams: the last row swaps Somya's frames for blank ones labelled "your team", so the reader sees the same method pointed at their workflow.

**Ending:** The final frame is empty, with a pencil circle around it and a caption in the serif: "Frame five is your team's workflow." Email and LinkedIn sit below it as two handwritten-style mono lines, and the last thing on the page is a red tick.
