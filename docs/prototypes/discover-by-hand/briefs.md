# Direction briefs: Friday's homepage

PROTOTYPE, throwaway. What reached Somya at the Direction gate in [What does Discover produce when one real Surface runs through it by hand?](https://github.com/somyanath/friday/issues/21).

**How these were made.** Four seeded agents all landed on one idea, which became Proof Sheet. A second round, where a script picked each agent's concept, palette, type, layout and motion, gave three more. Each Direction's rules come from research into the real tradition behind its look (`research/`). The surface brief is in `surface.md`.

**At the gate:** pick up to four to build. You can drop any, swap one in from the also-rans or the idea list, or combine two.

## A. Proof Sheet

**In one line:** the page is an editor's proof of itself: clean text, and every decision marked in the margin.

**Picture it:** uncoated cream ground, one teal-black ink, and a single vermilion used only for marks. A narrow flush-left serif column; a wide right margin holding terse marks. Nothing centred.

**Rules:**
1. The text column is set clean. All markup lives in the margin or on top of the type.
2. Every mark has two halves, one in the line and one in the margin, joined by position.
3. Marks are strokes, never fills. One marking colour, used for nothing else.
4. A mark always means something: strike cuts, caret adds, circle picks, stet reverses.
5. Margin notes are a word or an abbreviation, not a sentence.
6. The wide margin is the point. On a phone, notes fold under their paragraph.

**Named patterns:** dele, caret, stet, transpose (tr), close up, pilcrow, the "uncorrected proof" stamp.

**Signature moment:** at the Variant gate, four plain page thumbnails sit side by side. A vermilion circle draws itself round one, the others take a dele, and the margin reads "picked blind".

**Ending:** the last margin note asks "Where does your team's workflow slow down?" Email and LinkedIn sit below, closed by the page's only stet.

**Watch for:** warm paper and a red pen is the default skin for anything about editing, and it is where all four free-choice agents landed. It earns its place only if every mark carries meaning.

## B. Blueprint Turnstile

**In one line:** a cyanotype drawing sheet you walk sideways, and you can't pass a gate until you approve it.

**Picture it:** Prussian blue ground, white line work only. One enormous flush-left grotesk headline per station, tiny uppercase annotations, and a single white rail running the full width with numbered stations. Large empty blue, on purpose.

**Rules:**
1. Two inks, Prussian blue and white. Hairlines are thin white, never grey. No fills, shadows, gradients or images.
2. Three line weights: border, object line, hairline.
3. Scale carries the hierarchy: one huge size, one tiny size, nothing between.
4. Flush-left, ragged-right, on a visible grid. Asymmetric.
5. A real title block, bottom right (drawing number, drawn, checked, approved, date), holding true data.
6. Dimension lines sit outside the object with extension lines and ticks. One revision cloud, used once.

**Named patterns:** title block, zone grid, dimension and extension lines, leader line, revision cloud, revision block.

**Signature moment:** the horizontal scroll stops dead at each gate, a turnstile arm across the rail. A terminal line types `> direction gate: 14 ideas → pick 3`. The reader clicks APPROVE, the arm swings up with a tick, the title block's "approved" field fills in, and the scroll frees. On a phone, a thumb-sized APPROVE.

**Ending:** the last gate opens by itself. `> what slows your team's workflow?` waits with a blinking cursor, and email and LinkedIn are the end of the rail.

**Watch for:** "blueprint" is a default tech metaphor and a huge grotesk is a current AI-landing default; it holds only if the drawing conventions are honest. Blocking scroll can annoy a reader who skims.

## C. Two Staves

**In one line:** Friday as a score: Claude and GPT are two staves, and the critic's pencil corrects both.

**Picture it:** a sideways-scrolling manuscript on uncoated stock, in two risograph inks, fluorescent pink for Claude's stave and teal for GPT's, slightly misregistered. Huge italic Didone section titles set like tempo markings. Where the inks overlap they multiply into deep violet, the only third colour, and only where the two families agree.

**Rules:**
1. Two inks. Overlap multiplies (overprint), never a flat blend.
2. Misregistration of 0 to 3px that moves slowly or not at all.
3. Tone is grain, never a gradient. Solids stay slightly uneven.
4. Text never sits in a box: it sits on, above or below a stave, like tempo words.
5. The pencil is a third medium: grey, thin, uneven, not registered to the print.
6. One fermata per gate. Double barlines only at section ends.

**Named patterns:** fermata, double and final barline, rehearsal marks as section letters, tempo markings, cut bars, overprint and knockout, grain-touch.

**Signature moment:** as you scroll, the pencil strikes a note on one stave and the other family's note replaces it, in one slow stroke. At the Variant gate, four unlabelled notes stack under one fermata.

**Ending:** a final barline, then a blank stave headed "Your team's workflow?" Email and LinkedIn are two handwritten notes, one per ink, which overprint into violet on hover.

**Watch for:** neon riso with a serif is a familiar poster style, and the score is costume unless its bars carry real structure. Sideways scroll is hard to skim on a phone. It chose warm paper and a pencil on its own, so it is close to Proof Sheet in material but far in colour and energy.

## D. Pause Light

**In one line:** a calm control-room wall where one red lamp lights only where the human decides.

**Picture it:** matte black ground, bone text. One long left-aligned column of huge uppercase condensed statements, each followed by a slim telemetry strip (route, gate status, two-family check). Signal red, yellow and blue appear only as status. A PAUSE lamp is fixed to the top edge.

**Rules:**
1. Red appears only as the PAUSE lamp and gate markers. Never decoration, never a link.
2. Three signal hues plus bone on black. Every status chip has a text legend, so colour never speaks alone.
3. A lamp is off or lit: it flashes on arriving at a gate, holds steady until the human acts, and never loops.
4. One message per row in one condensed face (the DIN 1451 Engschrift class), uppercase for statements only.
5. Uniform strokes, square corners, no radius, shadow or italics. Tabular figures in readouts.
6. A lamp test on entry (every lamp lights once), then rest. Hazard stripes appear once, at the always-pause list.

**Named patterns:** annunciator tile, master caution, acknowledge, lamp test, hazard stripe, Engschrift, signal words.

**Signature moment:** in the Variant section, a draggable vertical wipe splits the screen: on the left, the same page agents make alone; on the right, a distinct seeded one. It sweeps once on entry, and the PAUSE lamp flicks red the moment the reader touches it.

**Ending:** the lamp goes steady red over "Your team's workflow: pause here." Email and LinkedIn are two condensed buttons.

**Watch for:** a dark dashboard with status colours and giant condensed uppercase are both common AI-landing tropes; it holds only with scarce red and real legends. ISO blue means "mandatory action", so label it if it stands for GPT.

## The same in all four

All four say the same things in the same order: the 162 rulings prove Somya builds tools, and the page ends on "your team's workflow". They differ in form, not in message. If you want Directions that lead with different messages, say so at the gate.
